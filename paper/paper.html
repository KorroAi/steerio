Abstract

Representation-level control, steering, offers an interface to a language model's internal activations that is measurable, monotonic, and adjustable, in contrast to the unmeasured, wording-sensitive effect of prompting. Methods such as Representation Engineering (RepE) and Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) are widely cited, yet controlled comparisons against prompting, systematic cross-model studies, and honest transfer evaluations remain scarce. We release steerio, a from-scratch, dependency-light implementation of RepE and CAA unified under a single "instrument" API (make_knob → play → read), and use it to run three controlled experiments on 5 real open-weight models spanning 4 families, all ≤1.5B on CPU.

Our contributions are reproducible rather than methodological:

  1. Steering vs. prompting (paired). Steering dominates on an instruction-tuned model (sentiment control power +0.535 vs. +0.409) and is more consistent: the +1 knob shifts 100% of evaluation prompts (sign test p=1.00) versus 85–92% for the best textual prompt.
  2. Systematic cross-model steerability. Sentiment control correlates 0.89–0.95 with amplitude on 4 of 5 families; a reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill) is barely steerable on sentiment (0.35); defensive refusal amplification is strong on Qwen (0.97; 50%→100%) and fails on TinyLlama, which has no baseline refusal to amplify.
  3. Direction transfer (negative result). Naive cross-family transfer via zero-padding fails; the transferred knob counter-steers (coefficient −0.21).

Two methodological findings matter for any reproduction: (a) instruct models must be evaluated in their native chat mode, base continuation annihilates the effect (0.000 vs. +0.548 on the same knob); (b) a true baseline requires an explicit empty knob set (knobs={}), because a None set plays every knob at full amplitude by default.

1. Introduction

Controlling a language model today means writing a prompt: an instruction whose effect is unmeasured, sensitive to wording, and ultimately obeyed by choice of the model itself. A different paradigm has emerged from interpretability research, steering vectors: directions in the model's residual stream that, when added at inference time, shift behavior along a concept (Zou et al.; Turner et al.; Arditi et al.). The promise is measurable, monotonic, adjustable control, an instrument rather than an oracle.

Three gaps separate that promise from practice:

  • No controlled comparison. Papers show a knob changes outputs, but rarely compare it fairly against prompting on the same prompts with the same external scorer.
  • No systematic cross-model study. Most demonstrations use one or two models. Steerability across architectures, sizes, and training regimes is largely unquantified.
  • No honest transfer study. "Steering vectors transfer between models" is often asserted; transfer with mismatched hidden sizes is seldom measured.
Contributions. (i) A clean, unified, from-scratch implementation of RepE and CAA under an instrument API. (ii) A controlled steering-vs-prompting evaluation on matched prompts and an external scorer. (iii) A systematic steerability benchmark across five models and four families. (iv) An honest transfer study that falsifies naive cross-family portability. (v) Two methodological corrections (native chat mode, true knobs={} baseline) that bear on the broader steering literature.

2. Related Work

Representation Engineering (RepE) (Zou et al., 2023) frames model internals as a space of interpretable directions that can be read (probes, projections) and controlled (direction addition). Activation Addition (Turner et al., 2024) reduces control to a single cheap operation without any optimization. Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) (Arditi et al., 2024) constructs a direction from the mean difference of positive/negative centroids and applies it at a chosen layer, finding that refusal is mediated by a single such direction.

Our contribution is not a new method. It is a clean, unified reproduction of both families under one API, and the controlled empirical protocol and results, including the negative transfer result and the chat-mode finding, that the field currently lacks.

3. Method

3.1 Steering vectors

A steering vector is a triple (direction, layer, scale). Given sets of positive and negative example inputs, the default raw contrast direction is

v = (1/|P|)·Σ h(l)(x) − (1/|N|)·Σ h(l)(x)

i.e. CAA with an unnormalized centroid difference. At generation, for every token we add s·v to the residual stream of layer l. Because v's norm is the centroid distance, s=1 is interpretable as "one full contrast." Three constructions are implemented: contrastive (CAA), pca (RepE; top principal component), and probe (RepE; logistic-regression weight vector).

3.2 The instrument API

from steerio import Instrument, prompts

inst = Instrument("openai-community/gpt2")                    # load a local model
inst.make_knob("positive", positive=prompts.SENTIMENT_POSITIVE,
               negative=prompts.SENTIMENT_NEGATIVE, layer=7)  # build a direction
inst.play("The book was", knobs={"positive": 2.0})            # steer generation
inst.read("I love this day.", name="positive")                # read a concept

play supports polyphony (several knobs simultaneously). Knobs persist via save_knobs / load_knobs.

3.3 Implementation notes

  • Compatible with GPT-2 (transformer.h), Llama/Qwen (model.layers), and generic causal LMs in transformers 5.x; runs on CPU.
  • All measurement uses external lexical scorers, never the steered model's own logits.
  • Reproducibility: greedy decoding, fixed seed, short max_new_tokens; every benchmark writes a timestamped JSON report under reports/experiments/.

4.2 Metrics

Control power (Exp. 1): the max absolute deviation of a condition's mean metric from the true baseline. Steerability (Exp. 2): Pearson correlation between amplitude and the external metric over five amplitudes (−2,−1,0,+1,+2), plus the slope. Transfer coefficient (Exp. 3): Δtransferred / Δnative.

4.3 Methodological controls

Native mode. Instruct models are evaluated with their chat template. In base continuation, the same knob yields 0.000 control on Qwen2.5-0.5B; in chat mode it yields +0.548 (Exp. 1). Reporting base-mode numbers for instruct models is therefore an artifact. True baseline. In steerio, knobs=None plays all knobs at amplitude 1; a valid baseline passes knobs={}. An early run used None and produced contaminated baselines; we corrected it. The committed numbers are from the corrected runs. External scoring ensures no self-referential artifact.

Consistency. Sign test, fraction of prompts moved in the intended direction: the Qwen knob at +1 moved 100% of prompts (p = 1.00), versus 85–92% for the best prompt. On gpt2, negative steering was consistent (p = 0.00, no prompt inverted), while positive prompting was not (p = 0.67).

Reading. Steering is at least as strong as prompting and markedly more consistent. On the instruction-tuned model it is clearly stronger, at near-zero marginal cost. Tone remains difficult to control by either method on these small models.

Steering directions are not portable across families by naive projection.

6. Discussion

  • Steering is real and measurable. Monotone, amplitude-consistent, and verified by an external scorer. On an instruction-tuned model it beats prompting on the dimension we measured.
  • But it is not universal. Tone is fragile on small models; reasoning models resist sentiment steering; TinyLlama has no refusal to amplify. Any product or safety claim must be model- and dimension-specific.
  • Transfer is the open problem. The cheap story ("learn once, apply everywhere") is contradicted by our zero-pad result. A learned cross-model mapping, or a representation provably aligned across architectures, is a genuine open research direction, not a solved one.

7. Limitations

  1. Models ≤1.5B on CPU; steerability on 7B+ models is unmeasured here.
  2. Prompting is wording-sensitive; we tested two formulations, not a systematic instruction search.
  3. Single-seed, greedy decoding.
  4. Lexicon scorers are crude proxies; absolute magnitudes are indicative, not calibrated probabilities.
  5. "Reasoning model resists steering" rests on one model and one dimension.
  6. The transfer design uses naive zero-padding; more principled alignment methods are out of scope.

8. Conclusion

We release a clean, reproducible steering library and an honest empirical picture: representation-level control works, often beats prompting on instruction-tuned models, and transfers badly across families. The negative results, transfer failure, DeepSeek's resistance, TinyLlama's refusal ceiling, are as informative as the positive ones.
